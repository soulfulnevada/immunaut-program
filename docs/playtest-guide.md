# Play-test guide: watching new players

Goal: learn where brand-new players get stuck, whether they understand why they failed, and whether they choose to play again. Watch 3–5 people, one at a time, without helping.

## Before each session

- Use the Netlify link, opened in a **private / incognito window**. That gives every player a fresh save, with no progress from you or anyone else.
- Plan 25–30 minutes. A laptop works best; a phone is fine if that's how they'd really play.
- Have this sheet and a pen ready. Sit beside them, not in front of the screen.

## What to say at the start (read it as written)

> "This is a game I'm making about vaccines and medicine. I want to see how it works for someone who's never seen it, so I'm not going to help. That's on purpose. Please think out loud: say what you're looking at, what you expect to happen, and anything that confuses you. There are no wrong answers. If you'd stop playing at some point, just tell me."

If they ask you something during play, answer: **"What would you try?"** Only step in if they're completely stuck for over 2 minutes, and write down where.

## What to watch for

Tick or jot a few words. Exact quotes are the most useful thing you can bring back.

| Moment | Watch for |
|---|---|
| Title screen | Do they find Play right away? Do they read the pitch? |
| Mission Control | Which tab do they pick first: Outbreak, Tutorial or Sandbox? Do they hesitate? |
| The lab | Do they read the part or drug descriptions, or click at random? Where do they pause longest? |
| Prediction (before a trial) | Do they guess thoughtfully, or skip it? |
| The 3D trial | Do they watch, speed it up, or skip straight to the end? Do they click anything to inspect it? |
| Mid-trial checkpoint | Do they understand the choice? Do they change the plan or just keep it? |
| Results / debrief | Do they read it? **Ask afterwards: "Why do you think that worked / failed?"** Can they say it in their own words? |
| After a failure | Do they try again on their own? Do they change the right thing? |
| Outbreak, chapter 1 | Do they run tests and try to read the evidence, or ask the lab tech every time? |
| Outbreak, chapter 2 (Pinecrest) | Narrow, broad or wait: can they explain why they chose it? |
| Town report | Do they read it? Does anything surprise them? |
| The end | **Do they keep playing or replay without being asked?** That's the strongest signal of all. |

## Questions afterwards (5 minutes)

1. In one sentence, what is this game about?
2. What was the most confusing moment?
3. Tell me about a time it went wrong. Why did it go wrong?
4. Was there a moment you wanted to try again? What made you want to?
5. Would you play it again tomorrow? Why or why not?

## Bringing it back

Paste your notes to Claude or Codex as they are, rough is fine. The most useful parts:

- the **three places** players hesitated or got stuck most;
- any failure a player **couldn't explain**, and what they thought the cause was;
- whether anyone **replayed on their own**, and what they were trying to improve.
